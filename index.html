Our story

It’s not enough to glue up a few hardwood plies, stuff them into a mold, cook it up, pop out a shell and expect to have the makings of a quality musical instrument. Drum shell building is an art.

It’s learned through understanding the value of age-old craftsmanship, along with years of woodworking experience, building shells and then refining your work. Unfortunately, much of the knowledge behind some of the best-made, best-sounding drum shells disappeared with the craftsmen who gave birth to them during the golden age of American drum building in the ’50s, ’60s and early ’70s.

Raised from the ashes of what was once C&C Drums’ reputable shell-building operation, a clean, modern workspace with expertly tuned equipment now houses EMCO’s production. Under the guidance of master woodworker Brian Anderson, the man responsible for developing those coveted original C&C shells, EMCO restores drum shell building to just that. An art.

Quality

Quality, not quantity.

Regardless of how many shells you need, maintaining the highest standard of quality is always at the forefront for us.

We’ve seen what happens when builders take their eye off the ball, and it isn’t pretty. Shells that crack, delaminate or warp are costly for both you and us. It’s a no-win situation.

We stand behind every shell we build.

You never have to worry about getting stuck with defective or questionable shells. Quality also means excellent customer service. At a time when you’re more than likely to find yourself working through questions about an order with an automated phone attendant, we still believe in having a friendly, knowledgeable person available to take care of your needs.

The goal

Only the best.

Our aim isn’t to become the biggest independent supplier of drum shells, only the best.

A good shell is the difference between a drum that sings and one that just drones.

It’s a complex science of understanding the characteristics of each wood, and how grain and ply direction, the amount of glue used, and wood density play a critical role in giving life to a shell. We also know the responsibility we carry in making sure your order gets to you when you need it.

What gives a shell its life

  • Grain & ply direction

    How each ply is oriented as the shell is laid up in the mold.

  • Glue

    How much is used between the plies.

  • Wood density

    The characteristics each wood brings to the finished shell.
